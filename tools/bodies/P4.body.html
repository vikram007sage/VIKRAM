01 / DECKS

Writing slides like a consultant

core
Pyramid principleAnswer first, then 3 supporting arguments, each backed by evidence (Barbara Minto).
Action titlesEach slide title is a full-sentence takeaway: not "Market size" but "Tier-2 cities will drive 60% of new card users by 2030".
Horizontal flowRead only the titles in order — they should tell the whole story.
One message per slideThe body proves the title; remove anything that doesn't.
ChartsPick by message: trend → line; comparison → bar; composition → stacked bar; relationship → scatter. Label directly, highlight the key bar, cite the source, avoid 3D and pie charts with many slices.
StructureExecutive summary → situation/complication → analysis → recommendation → impact and metrics → roadmap → risks → appendix.
Case-competition deck (10–12 slides): title, executive summary, problem and context, user/market insight (2 slides), solution (2–3), financials/impact, implementation roadmap, risks, conclusion; appendix for methodology. Bridgeway's consulting guide and the IITB casebook in your folder have examples.
02 / PRD

Product requirements document template

core
1. ProblemWho has it, how often, evidence (data, quotes), why now.
2. Goals and non-goalsWhat success looks like; what this release won't do.
3. Users and use casesPersonas or segments; top user stories ("As a first-time cardholder, I want…, so that…").
4. SolutionUser flow, key screens (wireframes), behaviour and edge cases.
5. RequirementsMust-have / should-have / later (MoSCoW); acceptance criteria for each.
6. MetricsNorth star, input metrics, guardrails; experiment plan (P2).
7. Dependencies and risksTeams, vendors, legal/regulatory (RBI, DPDP), technical risks.
8. RolloutMVP, phased launch, feature flags, support and communication plan.
9. Open questionsWhat still needs a decision, and by whom.
03 / TEARDOWN

Product teardown template

common take-home
1. Product and businessWhat it does, users, business model, key metrics, competitors.
2. Core user journeyScreenshots of onboarding and the main job; note friction and delight at each step.
3. What works and whyDesign and strategy decisions to praise, with reasons.
4. ProblemsRanked by impact on users and business, with evidence (app-store reviews, Reddit, your own test, a 5-user survey).
5. Recommendations2–3 prioritised improvements with mock-ups, metrics and an experiment.
6. Strategy viewWhere the product should go in 1–3 years.
Worked outline: teardown of a credit-card management app (CRED-style)

Business: users pay card bills and earn rewards; revenue from merchant offers, lending, commerce. Journey: sign-up, card linking via SMS/email parsing, bill reminder, payment, rewards. What works: trust signals, fast payment, reward gamification. Problems: rewards feel low-value (reviews), difficult to find statement insights, cross-sell prompts interrupt payment. Recommendations: (1) "spend insights" after each bill — metric: monthly active users viewing insights, retention; (2) move offers after payment confirmation — metric: payment completion time, offer CTR; (3) due-date auto-pay with salary-day timing — metric: % on-time payments. Strategy: become the default credit-health app as RuPay-on-UPI grows credit card use among young users.

04 / PROCESS

Doing a 48-hour take-home

TimeDo
First 2 hoursRead the brief twice; write the question in one line; draft a hypothesis and storyline (titles only)
Next 10 hoursEvidence: use the product, read reviews, talk to 5 users, gather data; size the opportunity
Next 8 hoursBuild slides/document to the storyline; mock-ups (Figma, or an AI prototyping tool)
Last 4 hoursReview against the checklist; cut 20%; proofread; export PDF
AI tools: using AI for research and mock-ups is increasingly accepted (and in some rounds expected), but verify every fact and number, and make the judgment your own. State your sources.
