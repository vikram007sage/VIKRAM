00 / ORIENTATION

Why companies buy companies

Strategic reasons

Growth (new markets, customers, products), capabilities and technology, scale and cost synergies, vertical integration, removing a competitor, diversification.

Financial reasons

Undervalued target, tax benefits, using excess cash, EPS accretion (weak reason on its own).

Why deals fail

Overpaying (winner's curse), synergies overestimated, culture clash, integration failure, leadership loss, too much debt. HBR's M&A collection in your folder covers these; studies commonly find most acquisitions fail to create value for the buyer.

Interview frame: "A deal creates value only if the value of the combined business exceeds the price paid plus integration costs: V(A+B) − V(A) − V(B) = synergies, and synergies must exceed the premium."

How a deal happens

1. Strategy + target screeningAcquirer (or its bankers) lists targets fitting the strategy.
2. Approach + NDAConfidentiality agreement; initial information.
3. Valuation + indicative offerNon-binding letter of intent / term sheet with price range (F2 methods, plus synergies).
4. Due diligenceFinancial, legal, tax, commercial, technology, HR, ESG — quality of earnings, hidden liabilities.
5. Binding offer + definitive agreementsShare purchase agreement, representations and warranties, indemnities, conditions.
6. ApprovalsIndia: Competition Commission of India (including the deal-value threshold for large tech deals), SEBI takeover code (an open offer is triggered on acquiring 25% of a listed company), RBI/FEMA for cross-border, sector regulators, NCLT for schemes of merger.
7. Close + integration100-day plan; synergy tracking.

02 / EPS

Accretion and dilution

most asked
Pro-forma EPS = (Acquirer NI + Target NI ± synergies − after-tax cost of financing) ÷ (Acquirer shares + new shares issued)

Setup: acquirer net income ₹1,000 cr, 100 cr shares (EPS ₹10), P/E 20× (price ₹200). Target net income ₹100 cr, bought for ₹1,500 cr (15× P/E). Tax 25%.

FinancingCalculationPro-forma EPSResult
All stockIssue 1,500 ÷ 200 = 7.5 cr shares; 1,100 ÷ 107.5₹10.23Accretive (+2.3%)
All debt at 8%Interest after tax 1,500 × 8% × 0.75 = 90; 1,010 ÷ 100₹10.10Accretive (+1%)
All cash earning 5%Lost interest after tax 56; 1,044 ÷ 100₹10.44Accretive (+4.4%)
All stock, target at 25× (₹2,500 cr)Issue 12.5 cr shares; 1,100 ÷ 112.5₹9.78Dilutive (−2.2%); needs ₹25 cr after-tax synergies to break even
ShortcutCompare the target's earnings yield (1 ÷ P/E paid) with the acquirer's cost of each funding source: cost of stock = acquirer's earnings yield (1 ÷ 20 = 5%); after-tax cost of debt = 8% × 0.75 = 6%; cash = 5% × 0.75 = 3.75%. Target yield at 15× = 6.7% → beats all three → accretive. At 25× = 4% → below the 5% cost of stock → dilutive.
CaveatAccretion ≠ value creation. A deal can be accretive and still destroy value if the price exceeds the target's value plus synergies.
03 / SYNERGIES

Synergies and the control premium

Cost synergies

Overlapping branches, headcount, procurement scale, shared technology. More reliable; usually realised over 2–3 years.

Revenue synergies

Cross-selling, new channels, pricing power. Less reliable; buyers often discount them heavily.

Valuing them

PV of after-tax synergies minus integration costs, phased in. ₹50 cr a year pre-tax from year 3, perpetuity at 12% WACC → roughly (50 × 0.75 ÷ 0.12) discounted 2 years ≈ ₹250 cr.

Premium: offer price over undisturbed share price, often 20–40%. Rule: the premium paid should not exceed the PV of synergies the buyer can capture, or the value goes to the target's shareholders.

What raises IRRLower entry price, more debt (if serviceable), faster EBITDA growth, higher exit multiple, earlier exit, dividend recapitalisation.

06 / PRACTICE

Questions to prepare

answer aloud
QuestionKey points
Walk me through a merger model.Purchase price → financing mix → combine income statements → synergies, financing costs → pro-forma EPS → accretion/dilution, sensitivities
Why use stock vs cash?Stock: acquirer shares overvalued, preserve cash/debt capacity, share risk; cash: certainty for sellers, no dilution, signals confidence
Is an accretive deal always good?No — value depends on price vs value plus synergies
Walk me through an LBO.Entry price and debt → project FCF and debt paydown → exit value → equity → MOIC/IRR
What makes a good LBO candidate?Stable cash flows, low capex, strong market position, cost levers, low existing debt, clear exits
Why does leverage boost returns?Smaller equity cheque; debt cheaper than equity; paydown transfers value to equity — but adds bankruptcy risk
What is goodwill in an acquisition?Price paid over fair value of net identifiable assets; tested for impairment (F1)
How are synergies estimated?Bottom-up by cost line; benchmarks from similar deals; phase-in and integration costs
Hostile takeover defences?Poison pills (rare in India), promoter holding, staggered boards; in India promoter stakes make hostile deals rare (L&T–Mindtree is a known exception)
What triggers an open offer in India?Acquiring 25%+ voting rights (or control) of a listed company under SEBI's takeover code
