01 / FOUNDATION

Adding and subtracting fast

Left to right

School taught right-to-left with carries. Your brain reads left to right, so add the big parts first.

47 + 38 → 40 + 30 = 70, then 7 + 8 = 15 → 85

Compensation

Round one number to a friendly one, then correct.

47 + 38 = 47 + 40 − 2 = 85
563 − 198 = 563 − 200 + 2 = 365

Subtract from 1000

Take every digit from 9, the last from 10.

1000 − 467 → (9−4)(9−6)(10−7) = 533

Great for change, discounts, "what's left" questions.

Add by counting up

For subtraction with awkward borrowing, count up from the smaller number.

812 − 579: 579 → 600 (+21) → 812 (+212) = 233

Near 100 (base method)

For numbers just below or above 100: cross-subtract (or add) the deviation, then multiply the deviations.

97 × 94: deviations −3, −6
97 − 6 = 91 | 3 × 6 = 18 → 9,118104 × 108: deviations +4, +8
104 + 8 = 112 | 4 × 8 = 32 → 11,232

The right part always has two digits (pad with 0: 96 × 98 → 94 | 08 = 9,408).

Squares you can do instantly

Ending in 5: n × (n + 1), then append 25.

65² → 6 × 7 = 42 | 25 → 4,225

Near 50: 25 ± the distance from 50 (hundreds), then the distance squared (2 digits).

47² → 25 − 3 = 22 | 3² = 09 → 2,209
53² → 25 + 3 = 28 | 09 → 2,809

Neighbours: 24 × 26 = 25² − 1 = 624.

Memorise once: squares 11²–30² (121, 144, 169, 196, 225, 256, 289, 324, 361, 400, 441, 484, 529, 576, 625, 676, 729, 784, 841, 900) and tables up to 19. They're on VIKRAM's flashcards.

Percent chunks

Build any percentage from 10%, 5%, 1%, 2.5%.

17.5% of 640 = 64 + 32 + 16 = 112
(10% + 5% + 2.5%)

Swap trick

x% of y = y% of x. Pick whichever is easier.

18% of 50 = 50% of 18 = 9

Successive change

a then b → a + b + ab/100

+20% then −10% → 20 − 10 − 2 = +8%. +20 then −20 is −4%, never 0.

Percent change

(new − old) ÷ old × 100

The base is always the starting value. 80 → 100 is +25%; 100 → 80 is −20%.

05 / TOOLKIT

Estimation and comparison

Round, compute, correct

4,872 ÷ 23.8 ≈ 4,800 ÷ 24 = 200

Both numbers moved down slightly, so the true answer is a bit above 200 (it's 204.7). In multiple-choice, 200-ish usually picks the option.

Compare fractions: cross-multiply

13/17 vs 19/25 → 13 × 25 = 325 vs 19 × 17 = 323

325 > 323, so 13/17 is larger. No decimals needed.

Growth over years

Rule of 72: doubling time ≈ 72 ÷ rate. For small rates, (1 + r)ⁿ ≈ 1 + nr + a little: 1.06⁵ ≈ 1.34.

Lakh–crore sanity

1 lakh = 10⁵, 1 crore = 10⁷. ₹48 lakh is 48 ÷ 320 = 15% of ₹3.2 crore.

06 / STRATEGY

Tactics for speed tests

80-in-8 style (negative marking)

  • A wrong answer costs a mark; an unsure guess is worth less than nothing.
  • Accuracy first for the first week of practice, then push speed.
  • Don't stall: if a question will take more than 15 seconds, move on if the test allows it.

Zetamac habits

  • Type the answer and move: the drill auto-advances on a correct answer.
  • Read the operation sign first (division hides as reversed multiplication).
  • Track a 5-day average, not your best score.

DI tests

  • Look at the answer options before calculating: often only one is in the right range.
  • Approximate with the fraction table.
  • Do easy questions first; mark and return.

Physical setup

  • Laptop, external mouse, quiet room, water.
  • Rough paper and a pen ready before the timer starts.
  • Calculator only if allowed; most aren't.

10 / RAPID RECALL

Last 10-minute look

Five multiplication moves

  1. × 5, 25, 125 → × 10/100/1000 then ÷ 2/4/8
  2. × 11 → digits apart, sum in the middle
  3. Near 100 → cross, then multiply deviations
  4. Ends in 5 → n(n+1) | 25
  5. (a+b)(a−b) = a² − b²

Five fractions

  • 1/8 = 12.5%
  • 1/6 = 16.67%
  • 1/7 = 14.29%
  • 1/12 = 8.33%
  • 3/8 = 37.5%

Three percent rules

  • Base = starting value
  • a, b → a + b + ab/100
  • x% of y = y% of x

Test day

  1. Accuracy before speed when wrong answers cost marks.
  2. Check the options before computing.
  3. Skip the 15-second monsters.
