00 / ORIENTATION

Three ways to value anything

Intrinsic (DCF)

Value = present value of future cash flows. Most rigorous; most sensitive to assumptions.

Relative (multiples)

Value = a multiple of a metric, based on similar companies or deals. Fast, market-anchored; inherits market mood.

Transaction / other

Precedent deals (include a control premium), LBO (what a PE buyer could pay), sum-of-the-parts, asset/liquidation value, VC method for startups.

Interview line (Damodaran): "Every valuation is a story told with numbers." Start with the story (why this company grows, how profitable it can be, what could go wrong), then turn it into inputs. Your folder has Damodaran's Little Book of Valuation and Investment Valuation, plus McKinsey's Valuation.
01 / BRIDGE

Enterprise value and equity value

core
Enterprise value = Equity value + Debt + Preferred + Minority interest − Cash (and non-operating assets)

Enterprise value

Value of the operating business to all capital providers. Pair with metrics before interest: revenue, EBITDA, EBIT, unlevered FCF.

Equity value

Value to shareholders = market cap for a listed company (share price × diluted shares). Pair with metrics after interest: net income, EPS, levered FCF, book value.

Why subtract cash?

A buyer of the whole business gets the cash, which effectively lowers the price paid; debt must be repaid or assumed, so it adds to the cost.

Classic question: "A company raises ₹100 of debt and holds it as cash. What happens to EV and equity value?" Equity value: unchanged (no value created). EV: unchanged too (+100 debt, −100 cash).
02 / DCF

The DCF, step by step

most asked
1. Forecast5–10 years of revenue, margins and reinvestment, tied to a story (market size, share, competition).
2. Unlevered FCFFCFF = EBIT × (1 − t) + D&A − Capex − ΔNWC = NOPAT − net reinvestment.
3. Discount rateWACC (section 3), in the same currency and inflation terms as the cash flows.
4. Terminal valueGordon growth: TV = FCFn+1 ÷ (WACC − g), with g ≤ long-run nominal GDP growth; or exit multiple (TV = EBITDA × multiple). Cross-check one with the other.
5. Discount + sumEV = Σ FCFt ÷ (1+WACC)t + TV ÷ (1+WACC)n.
6. Bridge to equityEquity = EV − debt + cash (± other claims); ÷ diluted shares = value per share.
7. SensitiseTable of value vs WACC and g (and vs margin); state what you must believe for the current price to be right.

Worked example (₹ cr)

05 / SPECIAL CASES

Valuing banks, startups and odd businesses

Banks and NBFCs

Debt is raw material, not financing, so EV/EBITDA and FCFF don't work. Use P/B vs ROE, P/E, dividend discount, or residual income. Bank with ROE 16%, Ke 13.5%, g 8% → justified P/B ≈ (16 − 8) ÷ (13.5 − 8) ≈ 1.45×.

Startups: VC method

Estimate exit value (e.g. year-5 revenue × exit multiple), divide by target return multiple (often 10–30× for early stage) → post-money today. Pre-money = post-money − investment. Ownership = investment ÷ post-money (F4).

Loss-making growth companies

DCF with a path to target margins (based on mature peers), a probability of failure, and large reinvestment early; or EV/Revenue cross-checked with growth and gross margin.

Cyclicals and commodities

Use normalised (mid-cycle) earnings; low P/E at the peak often means expensive.

Sum of the parts

Value each segment with its own method (e.g. Eternal: food delivery on EBITDA multiples, Blinkit on growth-adjusted revenue multiple), add, subtract net debt and holding discount.

Dividend discount

Gordon: P = D1 ÷ (r − g). D1 ₹10, r 12%, g 6% → ₹167.
