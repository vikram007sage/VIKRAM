Why the HR round decides more offers than you think

On campus, when two candidates clear the technical or case round, the behavioural round picks one. Interviewers are asking three things: Can you do the job? Will you do it (are you motivated)? Will we enjoy working with you?

Golden rule: every story must be true, and every number must be one you can defend for five minutes. Interviewers drill: "How did you measure 8%? What was the sample size? What did the other person say?" A made-up detail collapses under the second follow-up.
01 / FORMAT

STAR, and the follow-ups that come after it

core
S · SituationOne or two sentences of context. Where, when, what was at stake. (10%)
T · TaskWhat you were responsible for. (10%)
A · ActionWhat you did, in 3 steps, with "I" not "we". Include one judgment call or trade-off. (60%)
R · ResultNumber + what you learned or would do differently. (20%)

Keep the first telling to about 90 seconds. Then expect the drill, especially at McKinsey, whose Personal Experience Interview spends 10–15 minutes on one story:

Drill 1"What exactly did you say to them?"
Drill 2"What was the other person's view?"
Drill 3"What would you do differently?"
Drill 4"How did you know it worked?"
Drill 5"What was the hardest moment?"
Drill 6"Who disagreed, and why?"
McKinsey PEI themes: personal impact (influencing people who didn't report to you), entrepreneurial drive (creating something from nothing), inclusive leadership (getting a diverse group to work together), courageous change (pushing a change in an uncertain situation). Map one of your stories to each before any McKinsey interview.
02 / STORY BANK

Eight stories, drafted from your CV

fill in the real details

These are skeletons built from the facts on your June 2026 CV. Fill every [bracket] with what actually happened, then say each one aloud until it runs 90 seconds.

Story 1 · Impact with data — CheQ onboarding A/B tests (+8% conversion)

S: Summer 2025, PM intern at CheQ, a fintech credit-management app in Bengaluru. Onboarding drop-off was [X% at step Y].

T: I owned onboarding analytics and was asked to find and fix the biggest leak.

A: (1) Mapped the funnel and found [step] lost the most users. (2) Wrote 4 UX hypotheses [e.g. fewer fields, explain why we need PAN, progress bar, deferred permissions] and prioritised by expected impact × effort. (3) Ran A/B tests with [sample size, duration], agreed the success metric with engineering beforehand, waited for significance instead of stopping early.

R: 2 of 4 variants won and shipped: onboarding conversion +8%. Two losers taught me [what]. Lesson: pre-register the metric so nobody argues about results later.

Use for: impact, analytical, data-driven decision, "a time you used data to convince someone".

Story 2 · Influence without authority — aligning 3 teams on one sprint model (+25% experiment velocity)

S: At CheQ, design, engineering and analytics ran on different cadences, so experiments waited [N days] between teams.

T: As an intern I had no authority over any of them, but my experiments were blocked.

A: (1) Measured the delay and showed it in one chart. (2) Met each lead separately to learn what they needed [e.g. QA wanted test cases earlier]. (3) Proposed a shared sprint calendar, QA checklist and release cadence, piloted it for [2 sprints] before asking for permanent change.

R: Experimentation velocity up ~25%. [Who resisted and how you handled it.] Lesson: show the cost of the status quo in their terms, then pilot small.

Use for: teamwork, conflict, persuasion, McKinsey "personal impact".

Story 3 · Leading a large team — GES '25 with 76 people, 2,000+ delegates

S: E-Cell IIT Kharagpur's Global Entrepreneurship Summit 2025: 2,000+ delegates, 35+ CXO speakers.

T: As Associate Manager I led a 76-member team across logistics, content and operations.

A: (1) Split the team into pods with one owner each and a daily 15-minute stand-up. (2) [A crisis: e.g. a keynote speaker cancelled 48 hours before / venue clash] — I [what you did]. (3) Built a run-sheet and backup for every critical item.

R: Event ran for 2,000+ delegates; [metric: feedback score, sponsor renewal]. Lesson: [delegation / backups / communicating early].

Use for: leadership, pressure, managing a big group, inclusive leadership.

Story 4 · Initiative, alone — Entrepreneurship Awareness Drive, Ranchi '24 (1,000+ students)

S/T: I independently hosted the Ranchi edition: venue, colleges, speakers.

A: Negotiated with [N] colleges and speakers, [obstacle: a college backed out / budget cut] and [your response].

R: 1,000+ students reached. Lesson about negotiating when you have nothing to offer except the idea.

Use for: ownership, entrepreneurial drive, negotiation.

Story 5 · Persistence through failure — 500+ trials to get 20 Ω/sq graphene on polyimide

S: Research intern at the One Health Technologies Lab (Prof. Vinay Patel), building laser-induced graphene sensors.

T: Get reliable low-resistance graphene; early runs were inconsistent.

A: (1) Logged every run's parameters (power, speed, focus, passes). (2) [The point where it kept failing and what you changed in method, not just effort.] (3) Validated with Raman spectroscopy and 20+ cyclic-voltammetry runs, separating real graphene electroactivity from carbon artefacts.

R: 20 Ω/sq achieved and validated. Lesson: a failed run is data only if you record it.

Use for: failure/setback, resilience, attention to detail, "a time you were stuck".

Story 6 · Creative problem solving — Gaussian-process optimiser: 500 experiments → ~60

S: After 500+ manual trials, I realised brute force would not scale to new materials.

A: Built a Gaussian-process surrogate model with Kriging-believer batching to pick the next experiments, added transfer learning across carbon-similar materials, deployed it on Streamlit + Supabase for the lab.

R: Search for the optimum cut from ~500 to ~60 experiments. Lesson: step back and fix the process, not just the next result.

Use for: innovation, analytical, "a time you improved a process". Great bridge for analytics / AI roles.

Story 7 · Building from zero — co-founding KiFayti

S: Farmers pay ~₹2,000 per lab soil/water test; LIG sensors could do it for under ₹100 in the field.

A: Co-founded KiFayti; built 2 sensor products; wrote a ₹5 lakh IIT KGP grant application with pitch deck, projections, market sizing and GTM; mapped an IP roadmap.

R: [Grant outcome / cohort / pilot status — be exact]. Lesson about sizing a market honestly (SAM, not TAM).

Use for: entrepreneurial drive, ambiguity, "why should we hire someone who might leave to run a startup?" (prepare for that follow-up).

Story 8 · Team win under time pressure — Open IIT '25 Gold (Flipkart Minutes, 48 hours)

S/T: 48-hour case: expansion strategy for Flipkart Minutes in India's quick-commerce market.

A: [Your role in the team]. Built the 15-city, 2-model plan (dark stores + kirana partners), modelled a 13-month payback; [a disagreement in the team and how it was resolved].

R: Gold medal. Lesson: [e.g. pick the recommendation early and spend the rest of the time stress-testing it].

Use for: teamwork, structured thinking, "why consulting". Pair with the Indian Case Challenge (Bikaji, 500+ customer survey) for customer-research questions.

Still missing: a genuine failure where the outcome was bad (not "I worked too hard"). Candidates from your CV: a hypothesis at CheQ that lost, a sponsor or speaker who fell through at E-Cell, a grant or competition you didn't win. Pick one where you can name what you got wrong.

04 / OPENER

Tell me about yourself: three versions

core

Formula: Present → Past (2 proofs) → Why this role → Close. 60–90 seconds. Tailor the two proofs to the job.

Consulting / strategy

"I'm Aryan, a fourth-year dual-degree student in biotechnology at IIT Kharagpur. What ties my work together is solving messy problems with structure and numbers. Two examples: I won gold at Open IIT for a 15-city expansion plan for Flipkart Minutes, and at E-Cell I led a 76-person team to run the Global Entrepreneurship Summit for over 2,000 delegates. My research and my startup taught me to size markets and test ideas fast. Consulting lets me do that across industries, with the steep learning curve I'm looking for, which is why I'm here."

Product

"...I like building things people use. At CheQ, a fintech app, I ran four onboarding A/B tests and shipped two winners that lifted conversion 8%, and mapped 15 user journeys to fix friction that raised engagement 12%. I'm also co-founding KiFayti, where I'm turning a lab sensor into a ₹100 product for farmers, so I've seen product decisions from both a large app and a zero-user startup..."

Finance / analytics / fintech

"...I enjoy turning data into decisions. At IFSA I analysed 20+ HNI portfolios and benchmarked five startup IPOs on EV/revenue; at CheQ I worked on a credit-management app's analytics; and I built a Gaussian-process model that cut 500 experiments to about 60. This role combines the finance I've been learning with the analytics I'm best at..."

Don't walk through your CV line by line, start from school marks, or end without saying why you're in this room.
05 / HARD ONES

The questions a biotech dual-degree student will get

prepare word for word
"You're a biotech student. Why not core / research?"

Don't run down biotech. Make it a through-line: "Biotech taught me to run experiments and trust data. In my research and at KiFayti I found the part I enjoy most is deciding what to build and whether it can work commercially: sizing the market, the business model, the go-to-market. That's what this role is all day." Then a proof (Open IIT, CheQ).

"Your CGPA is 7.91. Why not higher?"

Brief, no excuses: "I chose to spend a lot of time on E-Cell, research and competitions, which is where I learned to lead and to work with real data. My grades in the courses closest to this role [Operations and Supply Chain, Entrepreneurship Essentials: EX] show what happens when I focus." Then move on.

"You're co-founding a startup. Won't you leave us?"

Be honest and reassuring: "KiFayti is a campus venture with my lab and co-founders; it taught me a lot, but I want to learn how great companies operate at scale before building anything of my own. That's why I'm here for [the internship / the next few years]." Never lie, but you don't need to volunteer long-term plans (UPSC, MBA) in an interview.

"Why this firm and not [competitor]?"

Two specific reasons: one about the work (a practice, a product, a client segment) and one about people or culture you learned from a real conversation ("When I spoke to [alum] about...") — the outreach module S6 gives you these conversations.

"What's your weakness?"

A real, minor-for-this-job weakness plus what you're doing about it: "I used to take on too much myself at E-Cell, which slowed the team. I now delegate by default and set a check-in rather than doing it." Avoid fake weaknesses ("perfectionism") and job-critical ones ("I'm bad with numbers" for an analyst role).

"Where do you see yourself in five years?"

Show ambition within the field: "Leading problems end-to-end, having gone deep in [fintech / consumer / healthcare], and helping others learn the way I'm learning now."

"Tell me about a time you failed."

Real failure, your mistake, what you changed, a later situation where the change worked. 70% on the learning.

Stipend / salary questions

For campus internships stipends are usually fixed; say "I'm comfortable with the standard offer for this role." For off-campus offers, ask for the range first; if pushed, give a researched range (Glassdoor, AmbitionBox, seniors) rather than one number.

07 / NEW FORMATS

One-way video and AI interviewers

Large recruiters increasingly screen campus applicants with recorded one-way video (HireVue and Indian platforms) or an AI interviewer that asks follow-ups. Recruiters or an AI scoring layer review them, often at speed with transcripts, so front-loaded, clearly structured answers do best.

Set-up

Camera at eye level, light in front of you, plain background, wired earphones, laptop plugged in. Do the practice question twice.

Answering

Answer first sentence first ("My biggest achievement was lifting onboarding 8% at CheQ."), then STAR. When ~20 seconds remain, jump to the result. Look at the lens, not your face.

Don't

Read a script (it shows), use filler slides, or use AI tools during the recording; many firms ban it and some detect it.

08 / CLOSE

Questions to ask at the end

Good

"What separates interns who get a return offer from those who don't?" · "What's a problem your team is working on that excites you?" · "How has AI changed how your team works in the last year?" · "What did your first six months look like?"

Avoid

Anything on their website. Salary, leave or work hours in the first round. "No questions."

Close

One line: "Thank you, this made me more excited about [specific thing]." Then a short thank-you email the same day if you have their address (check CDC rules first, see S6).
