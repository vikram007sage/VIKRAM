01 / STATEMENTS

The three statements in one minute

core

Income statement (P&L)

Performance over a period, on an accrual basis: revenue when earned, expenses when incurred. Revenue → gross profit → EBITDA → EBIT → profit before tax → net income.

Balance sheet

Snapshot on one date. Assets = Liabilities + Equity. What the company owns, what it owes, and what's left for shareholders.

Cash-flow statement

Actual cash in and out over a period, in three parts: operating, investing, financing. Reconciles net income to the change in cash.

Net income → top of the cash-flow statement and into retained earnings (equity)  ·  Ending cash from the CFS = cash on the balance sheet  ·  Changes in balance-sheet items drive the CFS
"Walk me through the three statements" answer: "The income statement shows revenue and expenses over a period, ending in net income. Net income flows into the cash-flow statement, where we add back non-cash items like depreciation and adjust for working-capital changes to get operating cash flow, then add investing and financing flows to get the change in cash. That ending cash appears on the balance sheet, and net income less dividends increases retained earnings, so the balance sheet still balances."

Why CFO ≠ net income: depreciation is a non-cash expense (add back); sales on credit raise receivables without cash (subtract the increase); buying inventory uses cash (subtract); delaying payment to suppliers keeps cash (add the increase in payables).
03 / FLOWS

Classic "what happens if" walk-throughs

most asked
Depreciation rises by ₹10 (tax 25%)

IS: EBIT −10, tax −2.5, net income −7.5. CFS: NI −7.5, add back D&A +10 → cash +2.5 (the tax saving). BS: PP&E −10, cash +2.5 → assets −7.5; retained earnings −7.5 → L+E −7.5. Balanced.

Company buys ₹100 of inventory with cash

IS: nothing. CFS: increase in inventory −100 in CFO. BS: cash −100, inventory +100. Balanced; no profit impact until the goods are sold.

Inventory of ₹100 is sold for ₹150 on credit

IS: revenue +150, COGS +100, PBT +50, tax −12.5, NI +37.5. CFS: NI +37.5, increase in receivables −150, decrease in inventory +100 → cash −12.5 (tax paid, cash not yet collected). BS: receivables +150, inventory −100, cash −12.5 → assets +37.5; RE +37.5.

Company borrows ₹100 and buys equipment

Day 0: IS nothing. CFS: capex −100 (CFI), debt +100 (CFF), net 0. BS: PP&E +100, debt +100. Year 1 (10-year life, 10% interest): IS D&A −10, interest −10, PBT −20, NI −15. CFS: NI −15 + D&A 10 = −5. BS: cash −5, PP&E −10 (net 90) → assets −15; RE −15.

Asset written down by ₹100

IS: impairment −100 (non-cash), tax −25 (if deductible), NI −75. CFS: NI −75, add back impairment +100 → cash +25. BS: asset −100, cash +25 → assets −75; RE −75. (In practice impairments are often not tax-deductible immediately; then a deferred tax asset is created instead — say so.)

Customer prepays ₹100 for next year's service

IS: nothing now (revenue not yet earned). CFS: increase in deferred revenue +100 (CFO). BS: cash +100, deferred revenue (liability) +100. Next year revenue is recognised and the liability unwinds.

Company issues ₹100 of shares and pays a ₹20 dividend

IS: nothing (dividends are not an expense). CFS: CFF +100 −20. BS: cash +80; equity +100 (share capital) −20 (retained earnings).

04 / CONCEPTS

Concepts that come up

Accrual vs cashRevenue when earned and expenses when incurred, not when cash moves. Hence receivables, payables, deferred revenue, prepaid expenses.
Working capitalCurrent assets − current liabilities. Operating (non-cash) WC = receivables + inventory − payables. An increase uses cash.
Cash conversion cycleDays inventory + days receivables − days payables. Negative CCC (customers pay before you pay suppliers) is a gift: q-commerce, supermarkets.
EBITDAProxy for operating cash before capex, interest and tax. Ignores capex and working capital — so not the same as cash flow.
Free cash flowFCF to firm = EBIT(1 − t) + D&A − capex − ΔNWC (used in DCF, F2). FCF to equity also subtracts interest after tax and adds net borrowing.
Deferred taxArises when book and tax accounting differ (e.g. faster tax depreciation creates a deferred tax liability).
GoodwillPurchase price above fair value of net identifiable assets in an acquisition. Not amortised under Ind AS/IFRS; tested for impairment yearly.
Capitalise vs expenseSpending with multi-year benefit (equipment, some development) is capitalised and depreciated; aggressive capitalisation inflates profits.
05 / INDIA

Ind AS and Indian reporting twists

TopicWhat to know
Ind ASIndia's converged version of IFRS, used by listed and large companies. Smaller companies use older Indian GAAP. Banks are moving to Expected Credit Loss provisioning from 1 April 2027 (B1).
Ind AS 116 (leases)Most leases go on the balance sheet: right-of-use asset + lease liability. Rent becomes depreciation + interest, so EBITDA rises. That's why Eternal and Swiggy report "adjusted EBITDA" treating rent as a cost (S5).
Ind AS 115 (revenue)Five steps: contract → performance obligations → price → allocate → recognise when each obligation is met. Matters for SaaS, construction, bundled offers.
Schedule IIIThe format Indian companies must follow for statements; "Other income" and "Exceptional items" are worth checking separately.
TaxDomestic companies opting for the concessional regime pay 22% base (~25.2% effective with surcharge and cess) — the 25% in our example.
Consolidated vs standaloneIndian companies publish both; use consolidated for groups with subsidiaries.
Promoter pledges, related-party transactionsDisclosed in notes; frequent red flags in Indian mid-caps.

07 / FORENSICS

Red flags in financial statements

Earnings quality

Profit growing but CFO flat or falling; receivable days rising fast (revenue booked, cash not collected); large "other income"; frequent exceptional items.

Balance-sheet games

Rising capitalised expenses; inventory growing faster than sales; large loans to related parties; contingent liabilities and guarantees in the notes.

Governance

Auditor resignations or qualified opinions, frequent changes in accounting policy, high promoter pledging, complex group structures.

08 / PRACTICE

Thirty technical questions

answer aloud
QuestionShort answer
Most important statement if you had only one?Cash-flow statement: shows real cash generation (though you need all three for a full picture)
Can a profitable company go bankrupt?Yes: if it runs out of cash (working capital, debt maturities)
Can a company with positive cash flow be loss-making?Yes: large non-cash expenses, customer prepayments, or financing inflows
How does inventory affect the statements?Buying: BS only (cash ↓, inventory ↑), CFO ↓. Selling: COGS on IS
Where do dividends appear?CFF outflow; reduce retained earnings; not on IS
Why add back depreciation in CFS?Non-cash expense that reduced net income
Difference between capex and opex?Capex: long-lived assets, capitalised; opex: expensed immediately
What is goodwill and when is it impaired?Excess purchase price; impaired when the acquired business is worth less than its carrying value
What are deferred tax liabilities?Tax payable later because tax expense on books exceeded tax paid now
LIFO vs FIFO in rising prices?FIFO: lower COGS, higher profit; LIFO not allowed under Ind AS/IFRS
What is working capital and why does an increase use cash?Short-term operating assets minus liabilities; growth ties cash in receivables/inventory
EBITDA vs operating cash flow?OCF deducts tax, interest (often) and working-capital changes
Why do companies like adjusted EBITDA?Excludes SBC, one-offs, lease effects; flatters results — always reconcile
How does stock-based compensation flow?Expense on IS (non-cash), added back in CFO, increases equity
Accounts receivable rises ₹50 — effect?CFO ↓ 50; cash ↓ on BS; no IS effect itself
Accounts payable rises ₹50?CFO ↑ 50; cash ↑
Debt repaid ₹100?CFF −100; cash ↓, debt ↓; interest falls next period
What is negative working capital good for?Funds growth with suppliers' money (supermarkets, q-commerce)
What's the difference between ROE and ROCE?ROE: return to equity holders (affected by leverage); ROCE: return on all capital
What is EV and how is it different from equity value?EV = equity value + debt − cash (+ minorities, preferred); value of the whole business (F2)
Revenue recognised but cash not received — where is it?Accounts receivable
Cash received but revenue not earned?Deferred revenue (liability)
What is a contingent liability?Possible obligation depending on a future event (lawsuit); disclosed in notes
How does a lease show under Ind AS 116?ROU asset + lease liability; depreciation + interest instead of rent
What's DuPont analysis?ROE = net margin × asset turnover × equity multiplier
What would you look at first in an annual report?Auditor's report, CFO vs profit trend, debt, notes on related parties and contingencies
How is a bank's P&L different?Interest income/expense are the main lines; provisions replace COGS; capital ratios matter (B1)
What is an impairment test?Compare carrying value to recoverable amount; write down the excess
Why might net income rise while EPS falls?More shares issued (dilution)
How do share buybacks affect statements?CFF outflow, equity ↓ (treasury shares or cancellation), EPS ↑ (fewer shares)
