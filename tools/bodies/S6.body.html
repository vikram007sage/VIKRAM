01 / GUARDRAILS

Rules to check before you send anything

must

Several IIT placement cells bar registered students from contacting the HR of companies in the campus process, and breaking that rule can cost you your placement registration. The rules differ by institute and year, so ask your CDC friends for KGP's current written rule. VIKRAM shows a reminder on the Outreach screen until you tick that you've checked.

Gmail limits: a free Gmail account is capped at about 500 recipients a day (To, Cc and Bcc combined), and a burst of cold emails can get it temporarily blocked. At 15 a day you'll never get close, which is the point.
02 / THE CDC LIST

Using the HR emails and numbers from friends

core

A list of thousands of HR contacts is tempting to blast. Don't. HR inboxes get hundreds of student emails, and a generic one is deleted in seconds. Treat the list as a map of where to dig, not a mailing list.

Filter it

Import the CSV into VIKRAM (Outreach → Import). Keep only companies in your six lanes. Mark everything else "Closed". A list of 3,000 should become 150–300 real targets.

Upgrade each contact

For each target, look the person up on LinkedIn. Is a hiring manager or team member in your lane findable? An analyst manager at the company beats a generic HR address almost every time. Use HR for process questions.

Keep it private

Contacts live in your browser and your private vikram-data repo only. Never commit the CSV to the public VIKRAM repo (it's already in .gitignore), never paste it into a chat, never share it.

Honesty rule: never claim a connection you don't have ("X referred me") and never pretend you met someone. "I'm a KGP student interested in your team" is enough.

04 / WRITING

Anatomy of an email that gets answered

core
SubjectSpecific and short: "IIT KGP student: summer 2027 internship, decision science". No "Request", no all caps.
Line 1Who you are in one clause: "I'm Aryan, a fourth-year dual-degree student at IIT Kharagpur."
HookOne line that could only be written to this person: their post, product, talk, a feature you used. VIKRAM won't let you send a cold email without one.
ProofOne sentence of evidence you can defend for 5 minutes in an interview. Numbers if you have them.
AskOne small, specific ask: "15 minutes this week or next?" Not "any opportunity".
CloseCV as a link (Drive, view-only), not an attachment. Signature with college, degree, year, LinkedIn.

Weak hook

"I have always been passionate about your esteemed organisation."

Strong hook

"I used your app's new spend-insights card last week and noticed it groups UPI transfers to friends as 'shopping'."

Length

Under 120 words for a cold email. If it scrolls on a phone, cut it.

Proof lines must be true. Your old CV had copied projects; module S4 rebuilds it from scratch. Until then, VIKRAM's proof lines are drafts: edit them in Outreach → Templates & setup so each one is something you actually did.
05 / PERSISTENCE

Follow-ups: where most replies come from

high yield

Sales-email studies keep finding the same pattern: the first email gets the most replies, but one or two follow-ups add a large share more, and few senders bother. Hunter's 2025 data found campaigns with follow-ups averaged a 4.9% reply rate against 3% without. These are sales benchmarks; a well-targeted student email to an alumnus usually does better, a generic one to HR much worse.

06 / STAND OUT

Send value, not just a request

For your top 10–20 targets, spend 60–90 minutes making something small and specific for them. This is the single biggest differentiator for a non-CS student, and each one doubles as a portfolio piece.

Product / consulting

One-page teardown of their onboarding or a feature, with three suggestions and how you'd measure them. Uses S2 (product sense) and S1.

Analytics / finance

A guesstimate or small analysis on public data about their market: "India's BNPL users by 2028, three ways". Uses S1 and A-modules.

VC / startups

A one-page memo on a competitor's launch, or three startups in their thesis area you'd look at and why.

07 / PHONE

The 60-second HR call

Use numbers from the list only where CDC rules allow and only in working hours (10:30–12:30 or 15:00–17:00, weekdays). Stand up, smile, have VIKRAM's script open.

  1. "Hi, am I speaking with [name]? I'm Aryan Rajak, a fourth-year student at IIT Kharagpur. Is this an OK time for 60 seconds?" (If not: "When should I call back?")
  2. "I'm reaching out about summer 2027 internships in [team] at [company]."
  3. One line of proof.
  4. "Could you tell me whether you take interns for this, and the best way to apply or the right person to email?"
  5. "Thank you. I'll email you my CV right after this call. What's the best address?" Then send it within 10 minutes, mentioning the call.
No answer? Don't call twice in a day. Send a short email instead: "I tried calling earlier about..."
08 / SYSTEM

The daily 30 minutes

MinutesDo
0–10VIKRAM → Outreach → Today: send every follow-up that's due
10–25Pick the next contacts, find a hook for each (LinkedIn, company blog, app), send until the day's count is done
25–30Update statuses for replies; book calls into Google Calendar

Quota maths: 15 a day is a ceiling, not a target. 5 excellent emails beat 15 rushed ones. Even 7 a day over 77 weekdays is about 540 first emails, plus follow-ups. At a few percent reply rate that's 15–30 conversations, which is what you actually need for 2–5 interviews.

Weekly review (Sunday): which template, lane and person type got replies? Do more of that. Reply rate under 3% for two weeks means the hook or targeting is off, not that you need more volume.
09 / LINKEDIN

LinkedIn in parallel

  • Profile first: clear photo, headline "IIT Kharagpur · Biotech dual degree · Product & analytics", About in 3 lines, the S4 CV projects as Featured.
  • Connection note under 300 characters (template "LinkedIn connection note"), always with a hook.
  • After they accept: thank them, then ask for 10 minutes of advice. Ask for a referral only after a real conversation.
  • Post once a week from January: a short teardown, a case you solved, a book note. It gives your hooks something to point to.
