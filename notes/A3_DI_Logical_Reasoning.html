01 / DI CORE

The DI toolkit

core

Growth

growth % = (later − earlier) ÷ earlier × 100

"From 2023 to 2025" → base is 2023. Year-on-year → base is the previous year.

Share and change in share

share = part ÷ total

A segment can grow in value while its share falls, if the total grows faster. DI loves this trap.

Ranking growth without dividing

Compare ratios later/earlier. 210/150 = 1.4 beats 520/400 = 1.3 at a glance. Use cross-multiplication for close calls.

CAGR approximation

CAGR ≈ total growth ÷ years − a bit

+21% over 2 years ≈ 10% a year (exactly 10%: 1.1² = 1.21). Rule of 72 for doubling.

Pie charts

Degrees ÷ 360 = share. 72° = 20%, 54° = 15%, 36° = 10%, 18° = 5%.

Line and bar charts

Read the axis scale first (thousands? crore? does it start at zero?). The steepest line is not always the biggest % change if the starting values differ.

Top three DI errors: wrong base year · confusing percentage points with percent · missing the units in the header (₹ crore vs ₹ lakh, '000 units).
02 / WORKED

A solved DI set

8 minutes

Quarterly unit sales ('000) of four brands. Illustrative data.

BrandQ1Q2Q3
Nova120150180
Orbit200180210
Pulse8010090
Quark100120160
Total500550640
  1. Overall growth Q1 → Q3? 640 ÷ 500 = 1.28 → 28%.
  2. Fastest-growing brand Q1 → Q3? Ratios: Nova 1.5, Orbit 1.05, Pulse 1.125, Quark 1.6 → Quark (60%). Orbit has the biggest sales but almost no growth.
  3. Orbit's share in Q2? 180 ÷ 550 ≈ 32.7% (1/3 of 550 is 183, so a bit under 33%).
  4. Average Q3 sales per brand? 640 ÷ 4 = 160.
  5. Did Pulse's share rise or fall from Q2 to Q3? Q2: 100/550 ≈ 18.2%. Q3: 90/640 ≈ 14.1% → fell by about 4 points.
Method: write the totals row first, then work every question from the ratios. Most options die before you finish dividing.
03 / LR CORE

The LR toolkit

  1. Read all clues once without solving. Note the type: arrangement, matching, ordering, selection.
  2. Draw the structure: a row of numbered seats, a circle of seats, a people × attributes grid.
  3. Place the certain clues first (fixed positions, "X is at the end"), then the relative ones ("immediately right of"), then the negatives ("not adjacent").
  4. Branch when stuck: write the 2 possible cases side by side and kill the one that breaks a clue.
  5. Answer from the final diagram, never from memory of the clues.
Circular tables: agree on what "right" means before you start. When people face the centre, a person's right-hand neighbour is the next seat going anticlockwise. Many puzzles avoid it by using only "next to", "opposite" and "between".
04 / WORKED

Three solved puzzles

Linear row

A, B, C, D, E sit in a row facing north (seats 1–5, left to right). C is in the middle. A is at the left end. B is immediately right of C. D is not next to A.

  1. C = 3, A = 1, B = 4.
  2. Left: seats 2 and 5 for D and E.
  3. D can't be next to A, so D ≠ 2 → D = 5, E = 2.
A · E · C · B · D

Who is at the right end? D. Between A and C? E.

Round table

P, Q, R, S, T, U sit at a round table (seats 1–6 clockwise; 1–4, 2–5, 3–6 are opposite). P is opposite S. Q sits next to both P and T. R is opposite Q.

  1. P = 1, S = 4.
  2. Q next to P: take Q = 2 (Q = 6 is the mirror image).
  3. T next to Q and not P → T = 3. R opposite Q → R = 5. U = 6.
clockwise: P · Q · T · S · R · U

Opposite T? U. Between S and U? R.

Matching grid

Amit, Bina and Chetan each like a different drink (tea, coffee, juice) and come from different cities (Delhi, Mumbai, Pune). Amit doesn't drink tea and isn't from Delhi. The person from Pune drinks juice. Bina is from Mumbai. Chetan drinks tea.

StepDeduction
1Chetan = tea; Bina = Mumbai (given).
2Pune drinks juice: not Chetan (tea), not Bina (Mumbai) → Amit = Pune, juice.
3Chetan gets the last city, Delhi; Bina gets the last drink, coffee.
CheckAmit: not tea ✓, not Delhi ✓.
05 / EASY MARKS

Quick-fire reasoning types

Syllogisms

Draw Venn circles; a conclusion follows only if it's true in every possible drawing.

All A are B; some B are C → "Some A are C" does not follow (the C's might sit outside A).

Blood relations

Translate step by step, write a mini family tree.

"He is the son of my grandfather's only son" → grandfather's only son = my father → his son = my brother (or me, if the speaker is male).

Coding–decoding

Find the letter shift. COLD → DPME is +1 each, so WARM → XBSN. Watch for alternating shifts (+1, −1, +1…).

Ranking and directions

Total = rank from top + rank from bottom − 1. For directions, draw axes and use Pythagoras for the straight-line distance.
