Structured Thinking

One skill, three lanes. Consulting asks it as a guesstimate, product asks it as market sizing or RCA, VC and finance ask it as "how big can this get?". Learn the method once, then drill one problem a day.

Why this module comes first

Every interviewer in your three lanes is testing the same thing here: can you turn a vague question into a clean structure, make sensible assumptions out loud, and land on a number you can defend.

The final number is the least important part. A wrong-but-structured answer beats a right-but-guessed one. Interviewers often change one assumption mid-way to see whether your structure absorbs it.

Where it shows up

  • Consulting: standalone guesstimate, or the market-sizing step inside a market-entry case.
  • Product: RCA + guesstimate round (Flipkart-style), TAM in the deck round.
  • VC: "size this startup's market" in a memo or interview.
  • Finance: revenue build-ups in a model; sanity-checking a DCF.

Exam-month routine

  1. Pick one question from the bank (section 9).
  2. Set a 12-minute timer; solve on paper, talking aloud.
  3. Do the sanity check by a second route.
  4. Open the hint, compare structure, tick it off in section 10.
01 / CORE · HIGH YIELD

The 6-step method

memorise
Clarify
Approach
Segment
Assume + compute
Sanity check
Summarise + so-what
  1. Clarify (≤1 min). Pin down geography, time unit, what exactly is counted, and why they want it. "Sold" vs "in use", units vs ₹, per day vs per year.
  2. Choose the approach. Demand side (who buys, how often), supply side (who makes or serves, at what capacity), or a proxy. Say why in one sentence.
  3. Segment. Break the population into 2–4 groups that behave differently (urban/rural, income, age, usage). Draw the tree before writing any number. Ask: "Does this structure make sense before I put numbers in?"
  4. Assume and compute. One assumption per branch, round numbers, a one-line reason each. Compute branch by branch, out loud.
  5. Sanity check. A second route (supply vs demand), a per-person or per-store check, or a comparison you know. If the two routes disagree by more than ~2×, find the broken assumption.
  6. Summarise. "So roughly X, driven mainly by Y; the most sensitive assumption is Z." Then add the so-what the interviewer can use.
Memory: C-A-S-A-S-S — "Clarify, Approach, Segment, Assume, Sanity, Summarise". The tree comes before the numbers, every time.
02 / CORE

Clarifying questions

Ask two or three, not ten. Each one should change your structure; if the answer wouldn't change anything, don't ask it.

DimensionAskWhy it matters
Definition"By smartphone, do we include feature phones?"Changes the base population
Geography"All of India, or urban only?"Decides whether you need an urban/rural split

Metric"Units or revenue?"Adds a price layer
Flow vs stock"Sold this year, or in use today?"Stock ÷ life = annual flow

If the interviewer says "your call": state the assumption and move on. "I'll take all-India, annual, in rupees, new sales only." Hesitating here costs more than a slightly odd choice.
03 / CORE

Pick the approach

Demand side (top-down)

Population → who uses it → how often → price.

Market = Users × Frequency × Price

Best for consumer goods and apps: shampoo, school bags, food orders.

Supply side (bottom-up)

Outlets or machines → capacity → utilisation.

Output = Units × Capacity/hr × Hours × Utilisation

Best for places and throughput: a petrol pump, a restaurant, an airport, a toll plaza.

Stock → flow

Annual sales = Installed base ÷ Life (yrs) + New adopters

Tyres, phones, fridges, ACs, spectacles.

Proxy / area / time

Use a known quantity that moves with the unknown: area for "golf balls in a room", hours for "flights a day", seats for "tickets sold".

Ask: "What physically limits this number?"

Rule of thumb: if the thing is bought by people, start from demand; if it is served at a place, start from supply; then use the other one as your sanity check.
04 / CORE

Segmenting the MECE way

MECE = mutually exclusive (no overlap), collectively exhaustive (nothing missed). Pick the split along which behaviour actually changes.

06 / TOOLKIT

Mental maths that saves minutes

Round first, adjust later

Round to 1–2 significant figures: 1.48 bn → 150 crore; 365 → 350 when it makes division clean, then say so.

Powers of ten

Write numbers as a × 10ⁿ, multiply the a's, add the n's. 6 × 10⁵ × 4 × 10² = 24 × 10⁷ = 2.4 × 10⁸.

Percentages

x% of y = y% of x. 18% of 50 = 50% of 18 = 9. 1/8 = 12.5%, 1/6 ≈ 16.7%, 1/7 ≈ 14.3%.

Rule of 72

Doubling time ≈ 72 ÷ growth rate (%). 12% growth doubles in ~6 years. Useful in VC and finance follow-ups.

annualiseper day × 30 = per month · per month × 12 = per year · per day × ~350 = per year
WORKED 1 / DEMAND SIDE

Annual market for school bags in India (₹)

consulting · VC

Clarify: all-India, new bags sold per year, in rupees, all price tiers.

StepAssumptionResult
School-age people (6–17)12 cohorts × ~2.4 crore~29 crore
Enrolled students~90% enrolment~26 crore
SplitUrban 35% / rural 65%9 crore / 17 crore
Bags per student per yearone bag lasts ~1.5 years → 0.67urban 6 crore, rural 11.4 crore bags
Average pricerural ₹350; urban 70% mass at ₹800 + 30% premium at ₹2,000 → ₹1,160
Revenuerural 11.4 cr × ₹350 ≈ ₹4,000 cr; urban 6 cr × ₹1,160 ≈ ₹7,000 cr~₹11,000 crore/yr
About ₹11,000 crore a year (~$1.3 bn), with urban buyers at a third of the volume but ~60% of the value. Most sensitive assumption: the 1.5-year replacement cycle.
Sanity check: ₹11,000 cr ÷ 26 cr students ≈ ₹420 per student per year. Plausible for a family buying one mid-range bag every 18 months.

So-what for a market-entry case: a new premium brand should target urban private-school students; that's where the value sits.

WORKED 2 / SUPPLY SIDE

Daily revenue of a petrol pump on NH-16 near Kharagpur

finance · consulting

Clarify: one pump, fuel sales only (no shop), a normal weekday, 24-hour operation.

  1. Capacity: 3 dispensers × 2 nozzles = 6 nozzles; one vehicle every 3 min at full use → 20 vehicles/nozzle/hr.
  2. Utilisation by time band: peak 6 h at 50% → 10/hr; normal 12 h at 25% → 5/hr; night 6 h → 1/hr.
  3. Vehicles/day: per nozzle 60 + 60 + 6 = 126; × 6 nozzles ≈ ~750 vehicles.
  4. Mix and litres: two-wheelers 55% × 3 L ≈ 1,250 L; cars 35% × 20 L ≈ 5,300 L; trucks/buses 10% × 100 L ≈ 7,600 L → ~14,000 L/day.
  5. Price: blended ~₹95/L (assumption; diesel-heavy) → ~₹13 lakh/day, ~₹48 crore/yr gross sales.
Sanity check that caught a mistake: a first pass with 8 nozzles, 70% peak use and 20% trucks gave ~37,000 L/day (~1,100 kL a month). If the interviewer says "a typical pump does a few hundred kL a month", your number is ~4× high: trucks and utilisation were the broken assumptions. Say this out loud and revise; it scores well.
Finance follow-up they love: dealer margin is a few rupees per litre, so ₹13 lakh of revenue is maybe ₹40–50k of gross margin a day. Revenue ≠ profit.
WORKED 3 / TRIANGULATE

Flights departing Mumbai airport in a day

asked: Deloitte, MakeMyTrip

Route A · runway capacity

Mumbai effectively runs one runway at a time.

  • Peak 6 h × ~45 movements/hr = 270
  • Normal 12 h × ~30 = 360
  • Night 6 h × ~10 = 60

≈ 690 movements → half are departures → ~350.

Route B · passenger demand

  • Assume ~5.5 crore passengers/yr → ~1.5 lakh/day
  • Half are departing → 75,000
  • ~170 seats × 85% load ≈ 145 per flight

75,000 ÷ 145 ≈ ~520.

The two routes give 350 and 520. Don't average blindly: ask which assumption is weaker. Peak capacity is probably higher than 45/hr and the night is busier than 10/hr, so the honest answer is "roughly 400–500 departures a day", with the range explained.
Why interviewers love this: two independent routes that roughly agree are far more convincing than one precise-looking number.

Pattern: in product rounds, end the guesstimate by pointing at the branch with the most headroom. That converts a number into a product insight.
07 / DELIVERY

Saying it aloud: the script

  1. "Just to confirm scope: we want X, for Y, per Z. Is that right?"
  2. "I'll approach this from the demand side, because it's a consumer good bought by individuals."
  3. "Let me lay out the structure first." (draw the tree; turn the page towards them)
  4. "Does this structure look reasonable before I put in numbers?"
  5. "For urban households I'll assume ~4 people, because…" (one reason per assumption)
  6. "That gives roughly N. Let me sanity-check it another way…"
  7. "So about N, driven mostly by A; the assumption I'm least sure of is B. If this is for entry, I'd focus on segment C."
08 / TRAPS

Mistakes that sink good candidates

Numbers before structure. Starting with "India has 150 crore people…" before saying where you're going.
Fake precision. "₹10,847 crore." Round it: "~₹11,000 crore".
Silent maths. Long pauses with no narration. Say each step.
Ignoring the hint. When the interviewer nudges an assumption, adopt it and recompute calmly.
Forgetting units. Mixing per day and per year, lakh and million.
No so-what. Ending on a bare number in a case or product round.
09 / PRACTICE

Practice bank: 30 questions

many from your folder's casebooks

Solve first, then open the hint. Questions marked with a firm name come from the "Previously asked guesstimates" list and the Communiqué Guessing Game in your folder.

Demand side

1. Market size of shampoo in India (volume, litres/yr)
Split urban/rural × sachet vs bottle users × washes/week × ml per wash. Rural sachet-heavy.
2. Baby diapers sold in India per year
Babies 0–2 (~2 cohorts) × usage share by income × diapers/day (high for affluent, occasional for others).
3. Spectacles sold in India per year (Gywnnie Bee)
Stock → flow: people who need correction × share who own glasses ÷ replacement life (~2–3 yrs).
4. Cricket bats sold in India per year (i3)
Players by age × bats per year; split leather vs tennis-ball bats, and clubs/academies vs casual.
5. Tyres sold in India per year
Vehicle stock by type × tyres per vehicle ÷ tyre life (km ÷ km/yr) + new-vehicle tyres.
6. Number of Excel users in the world (Microsoft)
Office workers globally × share using spreadsheets; add students. Revenue loss = users × share paying × price.
7. Annual market for formal ties in India
Working men in formal jobs × share required to wear ties × ties bought per year; add gifting.
8. Number of iPhones sold in India per year
Smartphone owners × premium share × iPhone share of premium ÷ replacement cycle.

Supply side / capacity

9. Daily revenue of a restaurant near Tech Market, KGP (High Radius)
Seats × table turns per meal slot × occupancy × average bill; add delivery.
10. Cars crossing a toll plaza on NH-16 per day
Lanes × vehicles/lane/hr × utilisation by time band; check against toll revenue if known.
11. Monthly cash withdrawn from the SBI ATM on campus
Users × withdrawals per month × average amount; cross-check with machine capacity per refill.
12. Number of petrol pumps in Delhi (Communiqué)
Vehicles × fuel per day ÷ litres a pump sells per day; or road length ÷ spacing.
13. Revenue of one multiplex screen per year
Seats × shows/day × occupancy (weekday vs weekend) × ticket price + F&B attach rate.
14. Number of cabs needed at Lucknow airport (High Radius)
Arriving passengers per hour × share taking cabs ÷ passengers per cab ÷ trips a cab can do per hour.

Proxy, area, time

15. Tennis balls that fit in a lecture hall (MagicBricks)
Room volume ÷ ball volume × packing efficiency (~0.6–0.7 for random packing).
16. Airplanes in the sky over India right now
Daily flights × average flight duration ÷ 24 h, adjusted for the time of day.
17. Daily flights from Delhi airport (MakeMyTrip)
Same as Worked 3: runway route + passenger route, then triangulate.
18. Length of roads in Delhi (Communiqué)
City area × share of land under roads ÷ average road width.
19. People who pass through Kharagpur station daily
Trains per day × average boarding/alighting per train; separate locals from long-distance.

Campus classics

20. Maggi packets eaten on campus in a month
Students × share who eat Maggi × times/week; add hall canteens vs room cooking.
21. People on campus using Instagram daily
Campus population (students, faculty, staff) × smartphone share × DAU rate by group.
22. Revenue of a hall's night canteen per semester
Residents × share who visit × visits/week × average spend × weeks in semester.

Product / VC flavoured

23. Daily active users of a UPI app in Kolkata
Smartphone users × UPI adoption × DAU/MAU ratio × app's share.
24. Quick-commerce orders per day in Mumbai
Households by income × q-commerce adoption × orders/week; check with dark stores × orders/store/day.
25. TAM for a sub-₹100 water-quality test kit in India
Households on borewell/untreated water × share willing to test × tests/yr; add B2G (panchayats) and B2B (RO dealers). This is your KiFayti pitch number.
26. Number of EV two-wheelers sold in India next year
Total two-wheeler sales × EV penetration; segment by city tier and use (delivery fleets vs personal).
27. Revenue of a coaching institute in Kota
Students × fee by course length; or classrooms × batches × students per batch × fee.
28. Number of LinkedIn users at IIT KGP and conversion to Premium
Students + alumni on platform × active share × premium conversion rate.

Hard / unconventional

29. Number of people who have ever lived on Earth (Deloitte)
Split history into eras; births per year × years per era. Tests comfort with orders of magnitude.
30. Probability a customer orders the exact same Subway sub as you (High Radius)
Product of choice counts (bread × protein × cheese × veggies subsets × sauces) with popularity weights.

Last 10-minute look

The six steps

  4. Assume + compute

Five anchors

  • ~95 crore internet users

Three formulas

  • Users × frequency × price
  • Units × capacity × hours × utilisation

  2. Two routes when you can.
  3. End with the lever, not the number.
