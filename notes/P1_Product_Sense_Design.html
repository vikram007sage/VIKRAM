Product sense
+ design

The product sense round asks you to design or improve a product in 30–45 minutes: pick the right user, find a real problem, generate ideas, choose one and define how you'd know it worked. This module gives you one flexible structure, the reasoning interviewers reward at each step, the new AI-assisted formats appearing in 2026, and six worked answers including Indian fintech and campus products.

00 / ORIENTATION

What product sense means to an interviewer

Product sense is judgment about what to build and why. Interviewers score: did you understand the goal, pick a meaningful user segment, find a painful problem, generate creative but feasible solutions, prioritise with clear criteria, and define success? Frameworks help you not forget steps; they don't earn points by themselves.

01 / STRUCTURE

One structure for design and improvement questions

core
1. Clarify the goal (2 min)Whose product? Which company — what is its mission and business model? Why would it build this now? Any constraint (market, platform, time)? Agree on the objective: growth, engagement, revenue, a new market.
2. Users + segments (5 min)List 3–4 segments by behaviour or need (not just demographics). Pick one with a reason: size × pain × fit with company goal.
3. Pain points (5 min)Walk through the chosen user's journey; list 3–5 problems; rank by frequency × severity; pick one.
4. Solutions (8 min)3 ideas: one incremental, one bold, one in between. Describe each as what the user sees and does.
5. Prioritise (3 min)Impact on the goal, effort, confidence, risk. Pick one; say what the MVP is.
6. Metrics + risks (5 min)North-star metric, 2 supporting metrics, 1–2 guardrails; risks and mitigations; how you'd test it.
7. Summarise (1 min)"For [segment], whose biggest pain is [problem], I'd build [solution], measured by [metric]."
Pacing: keep a visible structure (say "I'll go goal → users → pains → ideas → pick → metrics"), check in with the interviewer after segments and after pains ("Does that segment sound good to go deep on?"). Many candidates lose the round by spending 15 minutes on segments.
02 / USERS

Segmenting users and finding real pain

Segment by behaviour

"Students who split expenses with friends weekly" beats "18–24 year olds". Good axes: frequency of the job, motivation (save money vs save time), expertise (first-timer vs power user), context (commuter, small-town, family decision-maker).

Jobs to be done

People "hire" a product to make progress in a situation: "When my card bill arrives, I want to know if I can pay in full without breaking my month's budget." Frame pains as blocked jobs.

Pick with a reason

Size (how many), pain (how acute and frequent), underserved (do current options fail them?), strategic fit (does it move the company's goal?). Say the trade-off out loud.

Indian context points interviewers like: vernacular users, low-end Android phones and patchy data, UPI-first behaviour, trust and fraud concerns, family influence on decisions, tier-2/3 cities, cash-on-delivery culture. Use them only when they truly fit the prompt.
03 / IDEAS

Generating and choosing solutions

Idea generators

Remove a step; automate a decision; bring information to the moment of decision; add a social layer; change the default; borrow from another industry; use AI to personalise or summarise; partner instead of build.

Prioritisation tools

RICE = Reach × Impact × Confidence ÷ Effort. Impact vs effort 2×2. Kano: basic needs, performance needs, delighters. Use one, briefly.

Describe the experience

Not "add AI insights" but "after you scan the QR, the app shows 'This keeps you within your ₹8,000 food budget — ₹1,240 left this month' before you enter your PIN."

Design principles to name: reduce cognitive load (Hick's law: more choices = slower decisions), make the next step obvious, progressive disclosure, sensible defaults, feedback for every action, accessibility. Your folder has "Laws of UX" notes (20_SOURCES/PRODUCT_CONSULTING/Product).
04 / AI

AI product sense and AI-assisted rounds

new in 2026
Is AI the right tool?Use AI when the task needs judgment over messy inputs (text, images, conversations) and errors are tolerable or catchable. Don't use it where a rule or a form works better.
Design for errorsModels are probabilistic: show confidence, let users correct, keep a human in the loop for high-stakes actions (money, health), give sources.
Data + feedback loopWhere does the context come from (user data, documents, tools)? How do user corrections improve it? Privacy and consent (India's DPDP Act).
Cost + latencyEach model call costs money and time; design so the expensive model is used only when needed.
EvaluateOffline evals (test sets, graded outputs) plus online metrics (task success, acceptance rate, edits, complaints). See M3.
In AI-assisted roundsYou're judged on how you direct the tool: clear prompts, checking its output, deciding what to keep. Narrate your reasoning; don't let the tool decide your segment or metric.
05 / PRACTICE

Six worked answers (outline level)

try first
1 · Improve a credit-card app for young first-time cardholders

Goal: issuer wants healthy usage and fewer early delinquencies. Segments: first-job salaried 22–26, students with secured/FD cards, gig workers. Pick: first-job salaried (large, fast-growing, high early delinquency per bureau data). Pains: don't understand minimum due vs total due; lose track of spend across UPI-linked RuPay card; surprise interest. Ideas: (a) "pay in full" default with salary-day auto-pay; (b) live "spend vs budget" banner on every UPI-card payment; (c) explain-my-bill AI assistant in Hindi/English. Pick: (a)+(b) as MVP (high impact, low effort). Metrics: % of statements paid in full (north star for this segment), active usage, guardrails: spend per card, complaints.

2 · Design a product to help farmers test soil (KiFayti-adjacent)

Users: smallholder farmers, FPO coordinators, agri-input dealers, government soil-health programmes. Pick: FPO coordinators (aggregate many farmers, can pay, trusted). Pains: lab results take weeks; reports unreadable; no link to what fertiliser to buy. Solution: handheld test + WhatsApp report in the local language with a crop-specific fertiliser recommendation and dealer prices. Metrics: tests per coordinator per month, % of farmers acting on advice, yield/fertiliser-cost change in pilots.

3 · Improve Google Maps for two-wheeler riders in India

Segments: daily commuters, delivery riders, weekend tourers. Pick delivery riders (large, frequent, pain is time = money). Pains: routes assume car lanes; parking/entry at apartment complexes; rain and waterlogging. Ideas: two-wheeler routing that uses narrow lanes, community-reported waterlogging alerts, building-entry pins. Metric: on-time arrival, re-route rate, guardrail: safety incidents/reports.

4 · Design a product for the IIT KGP placement season (CDC)

Users: students, CDC coordinators, recruiters. Pick students. Pains: scattered notices, deadline misses, no idea which prep matters for which company. Ideas: single deadline feed with reminders, company-wise prep packs from seniors, mock-interview matching. MVP: deadline feed + reminders (VIKRAM-style). Metric: missed-deadline rate, weekly active students in season.

5 · Your favourite product and how you'd improve it

Pick one you use daily and can discuss deeply (e.g. a UPI app, Spotify, Notion). Structure: why you love it (user, job, what it does better than alternatives, a design decision you admire), then one improvement using the full structure, with a metric. Avoid products with no clear user problem to improve.

6 · Build an AI money assistant inside a UPI app

Goal: engagement + cross-sell (savings, insurance) without mis-selling. Segment: salaried users who overspend before month-end. Pain: no visibility until the balance is low. Solution: weekly AI summary ("You spent 32% more on food delivery than last month"), a "can I afford this?" check at payment, goals. Risks: wrong categorisation, privacy, pushing loans to stressed users — guardrails: opt-in, explainable categories, no loan offers when stress signals appear. Metrics: weekly active users of the assistant, % users meeting goals, opt-out rate.
