Levers

AOV up (bigger baskets, electronics, pharmacy), ad income up, discounts down, delivery cost down via density (more orders per rider-hour), wastage down via better forecasting.

Why new stores lose money

A new dark store runs at 200–300 orders/day for months: fixed costs dominate. So a chain opening stores fast shows worse margins even if mature stores are profitable.

Reality check (Q2 FY27)

Blinkit reported positive adjusted EBITDA of ₹184 crore in July–September 2026; Swiggy's Instamart lost ₹895 crore with contribution margin at −1.0% of GOV after briefly touching break-even in Q1. Zepto's IPO was deferred amid valuation concerns. Scale and density decide who wins.

04 / CUSTOMER

Customer economics

core
LTV = Average monthly contribution per customer × Gross-margin-adjusted lifetime = m ÷ churn (monthly)
Discounted: LTV = m × (1 + d) ÷ (d + churn)  ·  CAC = Sales + marketing spend ÷ new customers  ·  Payback = CAC ÷ m
HealthyLTV ÷ CAC ≥ 3 and CAC payback under 12–18 months (consumer) or 12–24 months (B2B SaaS).
Use contribution, not revenueLTV on revenue makes every business look great. A ₹600 order earning ₹34 contribution is a ₹34 customer-month, not ₹600.
Blended vs paid CACBlended includes free/organic sign-ups and flatters the number; ask for paid-channel CAC.
Cohorts beat averagesTrack each month's sign-ups: retention curves that flatten (some users stay for years) are worth far more than ones that decay to zero.
Drill: subscription app, ₹199/month, 70% gross margin, 6% monthly churn, CAC ₹900.

Contribution m = ₹139/month. Lifetime = 1/0.06 ≈ 16.7 months. LTV ≈ ₹2,320. LTV/CAC ≈ 2.6 (below 3). Payback = 900/139 ≈ 6.5 months (fine). Fix: reduce churn (annual plans, onboarding) rather than cut CAC; at 4% churn LTV ≈ ₹3,480 (LTV/CAC 3.9).

Drill: card customer, CAC ₹3,000, contribution ₹2,400/year, 15% annual attrition, 12% discount rate.

LTV ≈ 2,400 × 1.12 ÷ (0.12 + 0.15) ≈ ₹9,950. LTV/CAC ≈ 3.3. Payback ≈ 15 months. Sensitivity: if credit losses rise and contribution falls to ₹1,600, LTV/CAC ≈ 2.2.

06 / STRATEGY

Why profits last: moats

Network effects

Each user makes the product better for others: UPI apps (more payees), marketplaces (more buyers ↔ sellers). Two-sided networks need both sides; start with the harder side.

Scale economies

Fixed costs spread over more units: dark-store density, card processing, cloud. Leader's cost per unit falls faster than challengers'.

Switching costs

Pain of leaving: bank salary accounts, enterprise software, card reward balances.

Brand + trust

Amex, HDFC Bank, Tata: customers pay more or choose by default.

Proprietary data / tech

Underwriting data, recommendation models; durable only if it compounds with use.

Regulation / licences

Banking licences, RuPay's exclusive UPI credit linkage (S2 §7) — moats granted by rules can also be removed by rules.

Porter's five forces (rivalry, new entrants, substitutes, buyer power, supplier power) explains industry profitability; Hamilton Helmer's 7 Powers (scale, network, counter-positioning, switching costs, branding, cornered resource, process) explains a firm's edge. Use one, not both, in a case.
07 / EXAMPLES

Business models you should be able to explain

PhonePe: how does a free app make money?

UPI payments earn almost nothing (zero MDR for most merchant payments until NPCI's limited MDR framework from October 2026). PhonePe holds roughly half of UPI volume and monetises the user base through merchant soundboxes and payment gateway, lending distribution, insurance, stockbroking (Share.Market) and ads. Its IPO received SEBI approval in January 2026 but was paused in March 2026 amid market volatility, with reported valuation targets falling from ~$15 billion to $9–10.5 billion — a reminder that public markets price profits, not users.

Eternal (Zomato + Blinkit) vs Swiggy

Both run food delivery (profitable, ~₹3–6 of adjusted EBITDA per ₹100 of orders) and quick commerce. Blinkit scaled faster with more dark stores (target 3,500+ by March 2027) and turned adjusted-EBITDA positive; Instamart is still loss-making. The lesson: in density businesses, the leader's unit economics improve with scale while followers subsidise growth.

SBI Card: a pure-play card issuer

Revenue from interest (revolvers), fees and interchange; costs are funding, credit losses, rewards and opex. FY26: NIM ~11%, gross credit cost ~7.7%, ROA ~3.2–3.6%. The business is very sensitive to credit cost and cost of funds (B1).

Your own: KiFayti

Sell a sensor test at under ₹100 vs a ₹2,000 lab test. Ask yourself the investor questions: who pays (farmer, FPO, government, agri-input company)? Cost per test at scale? Sales cost per customer in rural India? Repeat frequency? This is exactly how an interviewer will probe it (S3 Story 7).

08 / PRACTICE

Practice questions

solve on paper
A food-delivery app has AOV ₹450, take rate 22%, delivery cost ₹55, discounts ₹20, payment cost 2% of AOV. Contribution per order?

Revenue 0.22 × 450 = ₹99. Costs 55 + 20 + 9 = ₹84. Contribution = ₹15 (3.3% of AOV). A ₹5 rise in delivery cost wipes out a third of it.

A dark store costs ₹5 lakh/month fixed; contribution ₹30/order. Orders/day to break even?

₹5,00,000 ÷ 30 days ≈ ₹16,700/day ÷ 30 ≈ 556 orders/day.

A SaaS firm starts the year with ₹100 cr ARR from 200 customers; expansion ₹25 cr, downgrades ₹5 cr, churn ₹8 cr. NRR? GRR?

NRR = (100 + 25 − 5 − 8) ÷ 100 = 112%. GRR = (100 − 5 − 8) ÷ 100 = 87% (below the 90% bar: churn needs attention).

Should a D2C brand with 40% gross margin, ₹800 AOV, ₹600 CAC and 1.5 orders/customer/year over 3 years keep scaling paid ads?

Lifetime orders 4.5 × ₹800 × 40% = ₹1,440 gross profit; minus fulfilment/returns (say ₹100/order = ₹450) → ₹990 contribution. LTV/CAC ≈ 1.65: no; fix repeat rate and AOV or move to cheaper channels (marketplaces, offline) first.

Why do quick-commerce firms push electronics and pharmacy?

Higher AOV spreads the fixed delivery cost; higher margin categories; differentiation. Risks: inventory cost, returns, regulation (pharmacy).

GMV grew 40% but revenue grew 10%. What happened?

Take rate fell: more discounts netted from revenue, mix shift to lower-commission categories, competitive cuts in commission, or more first-party sales recorded differently. Ask for the bridge.
