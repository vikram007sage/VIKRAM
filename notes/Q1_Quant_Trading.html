01 / TOOLKIT

Probability toolkit

core
Expected valueE[X] = Σ x · P(x); linearity: E[X + Y] = E[X] + E[Y] even when dependent — the most powerful trick in puzzles.
Conditional + BayesP(A|B) = P(B|A) P(A) ÷ P(B). Draw a tree or count equally likely cases.
Geometric waiting timeExpected trials until first success with probability p = 1 ÷ p.
Recursion / statesWrite E for each state and solve equations (coin patterns, random walks).
SymmetryLook for equivalent outcomes to avoid computation.
VarianceVar(X) = E[X²] − (E[X])²; for a fair die E[X²] = 91/6 ≈ 15.17, Var = 35/12.
CombinatoricsnCr, stars and bars, inclusion–exclusion, complements ("at least one" = 1 − none).

03 / TRADING

Market-making games

signature round
The setup"Make me a market on the sum of three dice" or "on the number of cars in Delhi". You quote bid (price you buy at) and ask (price you sell at).
Step 1: fair valueEstimate the expected value: three dice → 10.5. Range 3–18.
Step 2: widthWider when uncertain, narrower when confident or when competing: e.g. 9.5 – 11.5.
Step 3: updateIf the interviewer keeps buying from you, your ask may be too low — raise both quotes. If told one die shows 6, fair value becomes 6 + 7 = 13.
Step 4: manage positionIf you're long a lot, skew quotes down to attract buyers; think about worst-case loss.
Adverse selectionSomeone choosing to trade with you may know more; when a counterparty trades instantly, assume they have information.
Fermi markets

For unknown quantities (e.g. "weight of an elephant in kg"), give a fair-value estimate with reasoning (Asian elephant ~3,000–5,000 kg), then a wide market (3,000 – 6,000), and tighten as you get clues. Same method as guesstimates in S1.

04 / OPTIONS

Options basics they expect you to know

PayoffsCall = max(S − K, 0); put = max(K − S, 0).
Put–call parityC − P = S − K·e−rT (European options, no dividends).
At-the-money approximationC ≈ 0.4 × S × σ × √T. S = 100, σ = 20%, T = 1 year → ≈ 8.0 (Black–Scholes gives 7.97 at r = 0).
GreeksDelta (price sensitivity; ATM call ≈ 0.5), gamma (delta's sensitivity), vega (volatility), theta (time decay), rho (rates).
Intuition questionsHigher volatility raises both call and put values; a call's value rises with time to expiry; deep in-the-money call delta → 1.
Indian context: NSE is the world's largest derivatives exchange by contracts traded, driven by index options; SEBI tightened F&O rules in 2024–25 after finding most retail traders lose money. Good talking point for "why trading" and markets questions (F5).
05 / DRILL

Daily mental-maths drill

core

Speed

10 minutes a day on an arithmetic trainer (e.g. Zetamac-style: 120 seconds of +, −, ×, ÷). Track your score; aim for 50+ then 65+ (VIKRAM's OA milestone).

Tricks

Multiply near 100: 97 × 96 = (100 − 3)(100 − 4) = 9,312. Squares ending in 5: 65² = 6×7 | 25 = 4,225. Percentages: 15% of 240 = 10% (24) + 5% (12) = 36. Fractions ↔ decimals: 1/7 ≈ 0.1429, 1/8 = 0.125.

Probability daily

Two puzzles a day from "Heard on the Street" (Crack), "A Practical Guide to Quantitative Finance Interviews" (Zhou) or "Fifty Challenging Problems in Probability" (Mosteller).
