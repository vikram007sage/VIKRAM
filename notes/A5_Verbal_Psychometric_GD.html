01 / VERBAL

Verbal question types and fast methods

core
Reading comprehensionRead questions first (not options), then the passage once, noting the main idea per paragraph and the author's tone. Answers must be supported by the passage — reject options that are true in real life but not stated, too extreme ("always", "never"), or only partly right.
Main idea / titlePick the option covering the whole passage, not one paragraph.
InferenceMust be true given the passage; the smallest step beyond the text.
Sentence correctionCheck in order: subject–verb agreement, tense consistency, pronoun reference, parallelism ("to read, to write and to speak"), modifiers placed next to what they modify, idioms ("different from", "comply with"), concision.
Para-jumblesFind the opening sentence (introduces the topic, no pronoun pointing back), then link pairs (pronoun → noun, "however" → contrast, example → claim), then the conclusion. Eliminate options using one fixed pair.
Odd sentence outFind the theme of the majority; the odd one is on a related but different point.
Fill in the blanks / vocabulary in contextPredict the word before reading options; use signal words (although = contrast, moreover = continuation).
Critical reasoningIdentify conclusion and premises; strengthen/weaken by attacking the assumption linking them; assumption questions: negate the option — if the argument collapses, it's the assumption.
Timing: RC passage + 4 questions in ~6 minutes; grammar 30–45 seconds; para-jumble ~90 seconds. If you're past 2 minutes on one question, mark and move on.
02 / REASONING

Critical reasoning in practice

Argument: "Cities that added metro lines saw road accidents fall. So building metros reduces accidents."

Assumption: nothing else changed that reduced accidents (e.g. new traffic rules, helmet enforcement). Weaken: accidents fell equally in cities without new metros. Strengthen: the fall was largest along metro corridors.

Common flawExample
Correlation as causationIce-cream sales and drowning both rise in summer
Unrepresentative sampleA survey of app users about whether people like the app
Percentages vs numbers"Fraud rose 50%" from 2 cases to 3
False choice"Either we cut prices or we lose the market"
OvergeneralisationOne city's pilot proves national success
These flaws are also the questions interviewers ask in RCA and case rounds (P2, S2). Practising CR improves case thinking.
03 / PERSONALITY

Psychometric and situational judgement tests

What they measure

Traits like conscientiousness, teamwork, resilience, initiative, integrity and how you handle ambiguity. Many include consistency checks: the same trait asked in different words.

How to answer

Answer honestly, as you behave at your best at work. Read the role description first so you know which traits it values. Avoid extremes on every item, and avoid contradicting yourself — inconsistency is flagged more than any single answer.

SJTs

Pick responses that: address the issue directly, involve the right people, respect rules and ethics, protect the client/customer, and take ownership. Rarely correct: ignoring it, escalating immediately without trying, or acting alone against policy.

Example SJT: a teammate hasn't done their part and the deadline is tomorrow

Best: talk to them privately now to understand the problem and agree a plan; if it can't be finished, tell the manager early with options. Worst: do it silently and complain later, or report them without talking first.

Example SJT: a client asks for data you're not sure you can share

Best: tell the client you'll confirm, check the policy/manager, then respond. Worst: share it to keep the client happy.

04 / GROUP DISCUSSION

Group discussion playbook

core
What assessors scoreContent (facts, structure), communication (clear, concise), leadership (moving the group forward), listening (building on others), and behaviour (no interrupting, no aggression).
First 2 minutesUse the 1–2 minutes of thinking time to note 3 angles: economic, social, regulatory/technology, plus 2 facts.
Opening (if you start)Define the topic, propose a structure: "Let's look at this from users, businesses and government."
Mid-discussionAdd a new angle or a fact; build on others by name ("Building on Riya's point…"); bring quieter members in; redirect if the group drifts.
ClosingSummarise both sides and the group's direction in 30 seconds.
Speak3–4 times, 20–40 seconds each. Quality and timing beat volume.
Facts bank (from your notes): UPI ~25 bn transactions/month (Oct 2026); India has ~12.5 crore credit cards; RBI repo 5.50% after the October 2026 hike; 10-year G-sec ~7.2%; quick-commerce market leaders Blinkit, Instamart, Zepto. Refresh numbers in the week before a GD.
