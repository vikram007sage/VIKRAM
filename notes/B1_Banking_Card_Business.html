01 / BASICS

The bank balance sheet and P&L

core

Liabilities (where money comes from)

Deposits: current + savings (CASA, cheap, sticky) and term deposits (costlier). Borrowings: bonds, RBI repo, interbank. Equity: capital + reserves — the loss-absorbing cushion.

Assets (where money goes)

Loans/advances (retail, corporate, MSME, agriculture). Investments (mostly government securities, partly to meet SLR). Cash + RBI balances (CRR). Fixed assets.

The business

Borrow short and cheap (deposits), lend long and dearer (loans), manage the risks that creates: credit (default), liquidity (depositors withdraw), interest-rate (rates move), operational (fraud, cyber).

Net interest income (NII) + Fee income + Treasury gains = Total income

Rate hike: effect on a bank

Short run: loan yields on repo-linked books rise immediately, deposit costs lag → NIM widens. Medium run: deposits reprice, bond prices fall (mark-to-market losses on investments), credit demand cools, stressed borrowers slip.

Rate hike: effect on an NBFC / card issuer

Funded by bank loans and bonds that reprice quickly, while many loans are fixed-rate → NIM squeezed. This is why card issuers talk so much about cost of funds on earnings calls.

Basel III in one breath

Pillar 1: minimum capital for credit, market and operational risk. Pillar 2: supervisory review. Pillar 3: disclosure. Plus buffers (capital conservation, countercyclical, D-SIB surcharge for SBI, HDFC Bank, ICICI Bank).

04 / PAYMENTS

Payments: UPI, cards and who earns what

hot topic

UPI scale

25.47 billion transactions worth ₹31.53 lakh crore in October 2026, about 822 million a day; volume grew 23% year on year and the daily value crossed ₹1 lakh crore for the first time.

UPI economics

For years person-to-merchant UPI carried zero MDR, so banks and apps earned nothing directly; the government paid incentives. From 15 October 2026 NPCI began a revised MDR framework on select merchant transactions; small merchants (monthly UPI receipts up to ₹1 lakh) stay outside it, and over 96% of merchant volume is transactions up to ₹2,000.

Who wins on UPI

PhonePe and Google Pay hold most app volume. Money is made indirectly: lending, insurance, merchant devices (soundboxes), ads, and credit on UPI.

Credit on UPI

Since 2022 RBI has allowed RuPay credit cards (and only RuPay) to be linked to UPI. RuPay-on-UPI went from ~10% to nearly 40% of credit card transaction volume in about two years, but only ~8% of value, with average tickets near ₹1,000. RuPay's share of cards rose from ~3% to ~16%. No MDR applies to UPI-card payments under ₹2,000 at small merchants, so interchange per transaction is lower; SBI Card reports RuPay cards earn roughly 20–30% lower interchange but higher spend per card. NPCI's separate "credit line on UPI" has stayed tiny by comparison.

The big question to have a view on: UPI was ~77% of the value of digital merchant payments in mid-2026 and cards ~18%, down from ~20%. Is UPI a threat to cards (interchange) or a gift (card usage on QR codes everywhere)? Strong answer: both — volumes up, yield per transaction down, so issuers must earn more from revolve, EMI and fees.
05 / CARDS

Inside a card business

Amex · Capital One · SBI Card

Read S2 §7 first for the issuer P&L. This section adds what card teams actually manage.

Lifecycle levers

Acquire (channels: branch, digital, co-brand partner, salary accounts; CAC; approval rate). Activate (first spend within 30 days). Engage (spend per card, categories, EMI conversion). Retain (fee waivers, upgrades). Collect (DPD buckets, roll rates).

Co-brands

Partner brings customers and spend (Amazon Pay ICICI, Flipkart Axis, Tata Neu HDFC, PhonePe SBI). Issuer pays the partner a share of interchange/fees. Cheap acquisition, but thin margins and risk of losing the base when the contract ends.

Rewards

Funded from interchange and fees. Many unredeemed points lapse ("breakage") which improves economics. Devaluations (raising points needed) are the usual response when rewards cost too much.

Amex, Visa, Mastercard, Capital One–Discover

ModelWhoHow it earns
Four-party networkVisa, MastercardScheme and processing fees on volume; no credit risk; banks issue and acquire. Very high margins.
Three-party (closed loop)American Express, DiscoverIssuer + network + acquirer in one: keeps the whole merchant fee; premium spenders, fee-heavy model (Platinum), relatively low revolve.

Case study to know — Capital One + Discover: Capital One completed its roughly $35 billion purchase of Discover in May 2025, making it an issuer that owns a network. By mid-2026 it had moved about 25 million debit cards onto the Discover network (escaping the US Durbin cap on debit interchange, which applies on four-party networks) and begun testing credit cards on Discover rails, while keeping premium cards like Venture X on Visa because Discover's international acceptance is weaker. It is a live example of vertical integration, network economics and regulation in one deal — perfect for "tell me about a recent deal you followed".

Amex interview favourites: why Amex can charge merchants more (affluent spenders, closed-loop data, marketing to cardmembers); why Amex is more fee- than interest-driven; how an issuer would win millennials (travel, dining, lifestyle benefits, digital experience).
07 / RISK

Retail lending, credit risk and where stress shows up

Credit bureaus

CIBIL score 300–900; 750+ is generally considered good. Bureaus also feed alternative data. Lenders combine bureau, income (bank statements via Account Aggregator), and behaviour into scorecards.

Recent cycle

Unsecured loans and cards grew fast after 2021; RBI raised risk weights in Nov 2023; delinquencies in cards, personal loans and microfinance rose through FY25 and early FY26; credit costs have since eased. Younger borrowers (under 30) show higher early delinquency, and borrowers with several unsecured loans are the riskiest.

Secured is back

Gold loans were the fastest-growing retail segment in RBI's June 2026 Financial Stability Report as gold prices rose; home loans remain the biggest retail book.

Digital lending rulesLoans must flow between the borrower's and the regulated lender's accounts (no pass-through via the app), a Key Fact Statement with the all-in APR is mandatory, and default-loss guarantees (FLDG) from fintech partners are capped at 5% of the portfolio.
Expected lossEL = PD × LGD × EAD. Unsecured retail LGD is high (often 60–80%); secured (home, gold) much lower.
Vintage curvesPlot cumulative default rate by months-on-book for each origination cohort; a worse curve for recent cohorts = underwriting got looser.

09 / PRACTICE

Interview questions

answer aloud
How does a bank make money? (60 seconds)

It takes deposits and borrows, lends at a higher rate (net interest income), earns fees (cards, payments, wealth, processing), and some treasury income. It pays staff, branches and tech, and sets aside provisions for loans that may default. What's left after tax is profit; because banks are ~10× levered, a 1.5% ROA becomes a ~15% ROE.

Repo goes up 25 bp. Walk me through a bank vs a card issuer.

Bank: repo-linked loans reprice up at once, deposits slowly → NIM up near term; bond portfolio loses value; demand cools. Card issuer/NBFC: borrowing costs rise quickly, card APRs are already high and fixed → NIM compresses; stressed revolvers may default more.

You're given a card portfolio. What five numbers do you ask for first?

Cards in force and growth, spend per card, revolve rate, 30+/90+ DPD by vintage, and cost of acquisition per active card. Then interchange yield and rewards cost.

Why can Amex charge merchants more than Visa?

Closed loop: it captures the whole fee; its cardmembers spend more per card and are affluent, so merchants accept a higher fee for that customer. Amex also markets merchants to cardmembers (offers), giving value back.

Should a bank push RuPay credit cards on UPI?

Yes for acquisition and engagement among new-to-credit and young users in tier 2–3 cities, where QR acceptance is everywhere; but price it for lower interchange: modest rewards on UPI, push EMI and revolve, keep tight credit limits, watch early delinquency in new cohorts.

NPAs are at multi-decade lows. Is there any risk?

Yes: pockets of stress in unsecured retail and microfinance; RBI's own stress test shows GNPA could rise to ~3.8–5.1% under adverse scenarios; ECL from 2027 will raise provisions; a rate-hike cycle hurts weaker borrowers.

Pitch me one Indian bank stock in 2 minutes.

Structure: business (franchise, CASA, loan mix), growth (credit growth vs system), profitability (NIM, ROA, ROE vs peers), asset quality (GNPA, credit cost trend), valuation (P/B vs ROE), catalyst and risk. Pick one bank and fill it from its latest investor presentation.
