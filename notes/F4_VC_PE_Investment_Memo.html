01 / FUNDS

How funds work and get paid

core
StructureLimited partners (LPs: pension funds, endowments, family offices, fund-of-funds, SIDBI's Fund of Funds for Startups) commit capital; the general partner (GP, the fund manager) invests it. In India most funds are SEBI-registered Alternative Investment Funds (AIF Category I for VC, Category II for PE and debt).
Fees: "2 and 20"~2% management fee per year on committed capital; ~20% carried interest (share of profits) above a hurdle (often 8%), with a catch-up.
Life~10 years: invest in years 1–5, harvest in years 5–10. The J-curve: early returns negative (fees, losses written early), gains come later.
ReturnsMOIC/TVPI = total value ÷ invested. DPI = cash returned ÷ invested (what LPs care about most now). IRR = time-weighted annual return.

Power law: in a VC fund, one or two companies often return the whole fund. So each investment must plausibly return the fund on its own: a ₹500 cr fund owning 10% at exit needs a company worth ₹5,000 cr+ to return the fund.
02 / VC MATHS

VC method and term sheets

asked often
Post-money = Exit value ÷ Target multiple  ·  Pre-money = Post-money − Investment  ·  Ownership = Investment ÷ Post-money

Example: startup expected to be worth ₹2,000 cr in 6 years; VC wants 10× on a ₹20 cr cheque → post-money ₹200 cr, pre-money ₹180 cr, ownership 10%. If later rounds will dilute the VC by 30%, it needs 10% ÷ 0.7 ≈ 14.3% now → post-money ≈ ₹140 cr.

Liquidation preference worked example

VC invested ₹20 cr for 20% with 1× non-participating preference. Sale at ₹50 cr: preference ₹20 cr vs 20% × 50 = ₹10 cr → takes ₹20 cr; founders and others share ₹30 cr. Sale at ₹100 cr: ₹20 cr either way. Sale at ₹200 cr: converts, takes ₹40 cr. If participating: at ₹50 cr gets 20 + 20% × 30 = ₹26 cr; at ₹200 cr gets 20 + 20% × 180 = ₹56 cr.

03 / DILIGENCE

How VCs judge a startup

Team

Founder–market fit, speed of execution, ability to hire, honesty about weaknesses.

Market

Large and growing (TAM, SAM, SOM — bottom-up, not "1% of India"), timing (why now: UPI, ONDC, AI, regulation).

Product + traction

Retention curves that flatten, organic growth, engagement, revenue growth month-on-month.

Unit economics

Contribution margin, CAC payback, LTV/CAC (S5), burn multiple = net burn ÷ net new ARR (below 1.5 is good).

Moat

Network effects, data, switching costs, brand, regulatory licence, distribution.

Exit path

IPO (India's SME and main-board), strategic M&A, secondary sale to later funds.

04 / PE

How private equity creates value

Three driversEBITDA growth (revenue + margin), multiple expansion (buy cheap, sell at a higher multiple), and deleveraging (cash flows repay debt, so equity's share of EV rises). Buy-and-build (add-on acquisitions) combines all three.
What PE looks forStable cash flows, market leadership, low capex needs, margin improvement levers, management to back, a clear exit.
ExampleBuy at 10× EBITDA of ₹100 cr (EV ₹1,000 cr, 60% debt). EBITDA grows 8% a year to ~₹147 cr; some debt repaid (₹600 → ~₹534 cr); exit at 10× → equity ≈ ₹936 cr vs ₹400 cr invested → MOIC ≈ 2.3×, IRR ≈ 18.5% over 5 years. More detail in F3.

06 / MEMO

One-page investment memo template

core
1. RecommendationInvest / pass, amount, valuation, ownership, in one line.
2. Company in one paragraphWhat it does, for whom, business model, stage, key numbers.
3. Investment thesis (3 points)Why this market, why this company, why now.
4. MarketBottom-up size and growth; competition map.
5. Traction + unit economicsGrowth, retention, margins, CAC payback, burn.
6. Valuation + returnsEntry price vs comparables; exit scenarios (bear/base/bull) with MOIC and IRR.
7. Key risks + mitigants3–5 risks, each with what would reduce it and what you'd check in diligence.
8. Diligence questionsWhat you'd still need to verify before signing.
Worked example (illustrative): Series A in an agri-input marketplace for FPOs

Recommendation: invest ₹25 cr at ₹100 cr pre-money (20% post). Thesis: (1) ~10,000 FPOs being formed under the government scheme need input supply and credit; (2) the company's dealer network gives a lower CAC than consumer apps; (3) embedded credit via NBFC partners raises take rate. Traction: GMV growing ~12% month-on-month, 60% of FPOs repeat in 90 days, contribution margin positive. Returns: bull ₹3,000 cr exit (≈ 20× after dilution), base ₹1,000 cr (≈ 7×), bear ₹0–100 cr. Risks: credit losses on embedded lending (cap exposure, partner NBFC holds risk), seasonality, subsidy dependence. Diligence: cohort retention by state, credit-loss data, dealer exclusivity.
